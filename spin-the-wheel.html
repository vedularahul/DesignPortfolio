The short version

Researcher.Life sells an all-in-one subscription covering academic writing, research discovery, promotion and impact. The subscription page was already doing its job — clear proposition, plans, benefits, regional pricing for India. The brief was uncomfortable in a useful way: improve sales, but with no marketing budget. Everything had to come from people already on the page and about to leave without buying.

The moment we went after was exit intent. Conventionally that gets a discount banner, or a guilt-tinted modal asking whether you're sure. We built a spin-the-wheel instead — eight segments, real offers, and an outcome you don't control. Months free, and discounts across the annual, quarterly, two-year and three-year plans as well as the AI tool. The spin decides.

In the first sprint after release it brought in $4,231.17 in sales revenue, excluding Plus sales, on no additional acquisition spend.

Three principles doing the work

The mechanic isn't decoration. Each part of it carries a specific idea, and naming which one made the design reviewable rather than a matter of taste.

Variable reward

The wheel delivers a surprise. Not knowing what you will win is what makes the interaction compelling and memorable, in a way a fixed “here is 20% off” banner never is. After Nir Eyal on variable rewards.

Delighters

A cheerful, gamified moment in the middle of an ordinary exit is unexpected, and unexpected is the category of thing people remember and mention to someone else. After Kano's two dimensions of investment versus satisfaction, 1980.

Reciprocity

Offering a reward — even a small one — creates goodwill, and people who receive one are more inclined to reciprocate by exploring further or staying longer. After Nowakowska and Abramiuk-Szyszko, 2023.

A discount is a transaction. A discount you won is a story.

The part worth arguing about

Gamified discounting has a real failure mode: train people to wait for the wheel and you have taught your most price-sensitive segment never to pay full price. Some of the offers go steep, which makes that risk live rather than theoretical. The number above is one sprint. The honest long version includes what happened to full-price conversion over the quarters that followed.
