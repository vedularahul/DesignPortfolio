Cutting duplicate support tickets by 70%

Around 6% of all support tickets were duplicates — the same learner, the same issue, often within the same hour. Every one of them consumed support time that the original ticket had already claimed.

Reading a month of tickets in a spreadsheet

We took a calendar month of support data — more than 21,000 tickets from August — grouped it by unique learner, and went looking for repeats. The whole analysis ran in Google Sheets, which is worth saying out loud: the insight didn't need a data platform, it needed someone willing to read.

1,107 learners raised two tickets on the same day, producing 2,214 tickets of which 1,258 were duplicates. Another 123 raised three in a day. A further 181 raised three or more across multiple days.

We deliberately built the analysis on the two- and three-ticket groups. They're 85% of the affected learners, and people raising four or more are usually doing it out of frustration to force a response — real, but an outlier that would distort the pattern we were trying to see.

When the duplicates arrive

A ticket counted as a duplicate if it shared both the agent child category and the description with an earlier one. The timing is the interesting part.

Among learners raising two in a day, 98 arrived at exactly the same moment, and 724 within the hour — average gap of ten minutes. Ten minutes is not someone with a second problem. Ten minutes is someone who has no idea whether the first one worked.

Four reasons, and only one of them is a bug

  • 579 were people adding more information to something they'd already raised.
  • 330 came from replying to a ticket that had already been resolved.
  • 238 were raised because the previous one hadn't been answered.
  • 117 were generated automatically by a system bug.

The top ticket subjects were consistent — program extension, project and assessment extension, upcoming class schedules, class access, inside/outside policy, course completion and unlock criteria, project guidance and samples.

Then we called them

We phoned 25+ learners from across the dataset who had raised multiple tickets. Three things came back repeatedly, and none of them were about support being slow.

  • People didn't know they could reply to an existing ticket, so adding a detail meant starting a new one.
  • People didn't know the support ticket tracking page existed on the LMS at all.
  • People wanted to know when it would be resolved, and in the absence of an ETA, filed again.

One learner said plainly that they raised another ticket because they wanted to add more information. Another had raised one ticket that became several because of an internet issue.

Nobody in the research was trying to jump the queue. They just couldn't see the queue.

Four changes

The constraint was to build around the existing design rather than replace it, so the learner experience stayed intact. We walked the current flow from opening Help & Support through to the confirmation email and marked every point where the person is left without information.

  • Give an ETA at the moment the ticket is created.
  • Put ticket status on the Help & Support page itself, so an open ticket is visible before you raise another.
  • Put contextual information in the confirmation email — ticket number, LMS link, resolution time — plus a way to track it.
  • Change the experience for resolved tickets, so replying to one doesn't silently open a new one.

After rollout

Released in three phases. Duplicate tickets fell by around 70% overall. Those caused by system bugs went to zero, and duplicates originating from LMS, LMS Chat and Mobihelp dropped 100%. Tickets arriving by email and phone are still being monitored for a later pass.
