The short version

The research kept returning the same four complaints, in learners' own words: there were numerous steps to get to actual learning, progress wasn't clear, deadlines weren't clear, and nobody could tell them how to finish faster. One learner asked, in effect, for a timetable — tell me to finish this by here, then move to the next thing.

None of that is a teaching problem. It's a problem of a platform that knew where every learner stood and never told them.

The redesign went after the specific choke points rather than restyling the whole product: project submission, the live class, program induction, the community, and the navigation holding it together.

Project submission: five clicks and two scrolls to one

Submitting a project was the single biggest choke point in completion, and it took five sequential clicks and two scrolls to get through. The new flow stitches the whole thing together and gets it to one click.

Just as important, the deadline moved onto the course card itself, so the reminder lives where the learner already looks. Around it we designed the states nobody had designed before: awareness of the project mentoring session beforehand, the due date as it approaches, an evaluation ETA afterwards, certificate download and sharing, and a one-click extension request for anyone who missed the date.

Help & Support modelled what that could remove from the support queue — a large share of the monthly tickets on project evaluation, sample projects and extension requests were requests this flow answers by itself.

Designing around the live class

The live virtual class is the moment with the most value and the most ways to miss it. The redesign put the most important thing on screen first at each stage: when the class is and what to read beforehand, a one-click join when it's about to start, and afterwards the recording — not just as a download, but playable in snippets by marked topic, so catching up doesn't mean re-watching two hours.

Making the cohort visible

Around 60% of learners said they would like to interact and learn with peers. The platform gave them almost nowhere to do it, so the design put peers where the learning already was: discussion attached to each self-learning video, live counts of how many people are in a lesson right now, and an induction flow that prompts a learner to introduce themselves to their cohort, add it to their calendar, and join a later session if they miss it.

Seeing that fifty other people are stuck on the same lesson is worth more than any nudge email.

Where it landed

Completion rates rose 22%, and the proportion of learners who actually upskilled went from 13% to 30%.

The page all of this hangs off — the program details page, which is really the learning section — got its own redesign. That is written up in the program details page redesign.
